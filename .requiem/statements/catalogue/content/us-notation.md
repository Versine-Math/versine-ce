Every student-facing string (prompts, explanations, blurbs, visual labels, hints) uses US notation and US word choice: money in dollars and cents ($4.50, 75¢, with US prices that are believable), never £, € or pence; US spelling (center, meter, liter, color, favorite, gray; candy not sweets); US classroom terms (parentheses, least common multiple, slope, distribute; students, not pupils); a decimal point and comma thousands. Versine's students are in the US and its levels are mapped to Common Core and TEKS, and an unfamiliar currency, spelling or term is a second thing to decode on top of the math, which a drill should never ask. Code comments and docs follow the same wording, because the repository is public and foreign wording can damage a family's trust; identifiers, slugs and file names keep their old spelling, since renaming them would break links and stored progress.
